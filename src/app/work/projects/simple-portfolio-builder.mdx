I’m Tolga, a software developer interested in digital literacy. Today we’ll discuss how AI tools are transforming entry-level positions in the software industry.

## Are junior positions really shrinking?

Whenever I say “new graduate engineer,” my friends and people around me get annoyed 😊 So I’ll use “junior” instead. Enjoy the article.

For the past few years, we have often heard the claim that junior software developer positions are no longer as common as they used to be. Several concrete developments support this idea. AI coding tools such as Copilot, Cursor, and Claude can now complete routine CRUD work, boilerplate code, and simple debugging tasks in minutes—tasks that once took a junior developer hours.

This means a company may be able to handle work previously divided among several juniors with one mid-level developer using these tools. Global economic uncertainty has also reduced companies’ willingness to invest in positions that require significant training. As a result, many technology companies have frozen or reduced entry-level hiring.

## But the picture is not as bleak as it looks

Many recruiters still say they struggle to find candidates with the qualities they need. The issue is not simply that there are no jobs; it is that there are fewer candidates with the right profile. That raises an important question: which skills help a junior developer stand out in the AI era?

## Which skills matter most?

**The ability to evaluate code.** In the past, “How much code can you write?” was often decisive. Now, “How well do you understand code that has been written or generated?” is becoming more important. AI can produce a function in seconds, but predicting when that function will fail still requires human judgment.

**Strong computer science fundamentals.** Understanding data structures, algorithmic complexity, and system design is increasingly important for evaluating solutions produced by AI tools.

**End-to-end project experience.** Planning, developing, testing, deploying, and monitoring a project provides a clear advantage over producing isolated code snippets.

**Effective use of AI tools.** Refusing to use these tools and leaving everything to them are both disadvantages. Companies want people who can use AI as an assistant while questioning its output.

## What do companies look for in junior candidates?

Hiring surveys suggest that technology companies are giving more weight to three things:

1. **A concrete, working portfolio** — more convincing evidence than a single line on a CV.
2. **Depth in a niche** — showing practical expertise in an area such as frontend performance, security, or data engineering.
3. **A problem-solving approach** — interviews increasingly evaluate how candidates approach a problem rather than whether they memorized a standard solution.

## Conclusion

AI is definitely changing the nature of entry-level software roles. But this should not be interpreted as “junior roles have disappeared completely.” The industry is moving toward people who understand both how to write code and how to evaluate it. Strong fundamentals, real projects, and conscious use of AI remain the most reliable ways to stand out.

I'm Tolga, a software developer with an interest in digital literacy. Today we're talking about LinkedIn's own peculiar language — the kind everyone recognizes by heart but nobody ever questions.

## LinkedIn has its own parallel universe

Open LinkedIn and most of what you see is written in a language nobody actually speaks in everyday life. No one calls a friend their "valued connection." No one texts a coworker "dear contact." Yet the moment people log onto the platform, everyone suddenly starts using the same vocabulary. This isn't a coincidence — it's a language shaped jointly by the algorithm and the platform's culture.

## The most common clichés

- **"I couldn't resist sharing this."** What usually follows is someone getting a certificate or attending a meeting. The gap between the emotional weight of the opener and how mundane the actual content is happens to be the clearest signature of this cliché.
- **"I want to humbly share that..."** The sentence that follows is almost never humble — it's typically an award, a promotion, or an achievement announcement. This pattern, known as the "humblebrag," is an attempt to wrap bragging in a modest frame.
- **"My valued connections, dear contacts..."** These greeting formulas, used when it's unclear who's actually reading the post, claim intimacy but often create the opposite effect — distance.
- **"What do you think about this? Let's discuss in the comments 👇"** Rather than genuinely opening a discussion, this typically functions as a call to feed the platform's engagement algorithm.
- **Buzzword piles that carry no real meaning.** Words like "synergy," "adding value," "ecosystem," and "insight" are often used not to convey concrete information but simply to create a sense of seriousness.

## Why did this language spread so widely?

A few mechanisms are behind it:

1. **The algorithm rewards engagement.** Emotional or provocative content tends to get more comments and shares, so the platform surfaces these posts to more people — which in turn pushes users toward similar language.
2. **The search for social proof.** In an environment where everyone uses the same formulas, writing differently can feel risky, as if it signals a lack of "professionalism."
3. **Personal branding pressure.** The idea of "building a personal brand" pushes many users toward an inauthentic, performative style.

## But does this actually cause a problem?

Short answer: yes, in a few ways. First, this language buries genuine information under noise — readers increasingly struggle to tell a sincere observation apart from a performative post. Second, constantly consuming these formulas breeds fatigue and distrust; research suggests users are growing more skeptical of exaggerated "success story" content. Third, this style actually makes communication less efficient — burying a piece of information under three flowery paragraphs wastes the reader's time for no good reason.

## So what's the alternative?

Avoiding clichés doesn't mean writing coldly or without warmth. On the contrary:

- Opening with a direct observation or piece of information is often far more effective than "I couldn't resist sharing this."
- When sharing an achievement, explaining concretely why it matters is more convincing than empty self-praise.
- Before reaching for a buzzword, it's usually enough to ask whether that word actually adds meaning to the sentence.

## Conclusion

LinkedIn's clichéd language is a natural byproduct of the platform's own dynamics. But that doesn't mean everyone has to adopt the same pretentious tone. Sometimes the most attention-grabbing post is the one that skips "my valued connections" altogether and simply opens with a clear, sincere sentence.
